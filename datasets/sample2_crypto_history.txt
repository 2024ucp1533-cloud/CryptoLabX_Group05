Cryptography is the practice of secure communication in the presence of adversaries.
For centuries, rulers and generals relied on simple substitution and transposition
ciphers to protect their messages. The Caesar cipher, named after Julius Caesar,
shifted each letter of the alphabet by a fixed number of positions. Although easy to
break by modern standards, it laid the groundwork for more advanced techniques.
During the twentieth century, machines such as the Enigma introduced mechanical and
electromechanical encryption, prompting the development of early computational
cryptanalysis. Today, cryptography underpins nearly every secure digital system,
from online banking to private messaging.
